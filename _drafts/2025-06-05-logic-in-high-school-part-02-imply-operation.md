The [IMPLY operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_conditional) might have been 
the greatest bottleneck in my learning.
Particularly, the most confusing one 
is the concept of [Vacuous truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuous_truth).
As a result I had to treat it as yet another 
math operation that was provided out-of-the-box,
ready to memorize, and invoke 
whenever I saw a boolean expression. 
This **take it or leave it** mentality is never productive. 
Also the subject on *Set Theory* and *Logic* 
went by in a flash, compared to subjects 
like differentials and integrals. 
After I set aside some brain cells
to do the take-it-or-leave-it, the brain cells
did not have the chance to synthesize
another way of understanding it.
So it forever remained as take-it-or-leave-it
at the back of my mind.  

I remember the tutor touched on [Modus ponens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modus_ponens) 
during one of the cram school study sessions. 
An inference technique that, as far as I could recall, 
was not even taught by 
the Math teachers in a classroom setting in high school. 
Or perhaps it was touched upon briefly, 
but I was asleep during class.

Just like with most cram schools everywhere else, 
people focused on studying up on materials 
for university entrance exam. 
So helping a student create a deep understanding 
could have been deemed not too productive. 
That, and generally I was just too lazy.
Either way, it is a debt that has to be paid off. 

Really, I could have learned so much
if I had just been aware of
the Wikipedia article on Euler diagram.
I tried looking at the [history entries of that Wikipedia page](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Euler_diagram&action=history&offset=&limit=500). 
It had already existed during my high school days!
I feel like I could have gained so much brain cells
if I had just been aware of how the operation had worked.
